The problem

A part is late. A line stops. A human works the phones.

what actually happens

The buyer finds the shortage in a spreadsheet, calls four suppliers, writes the answers in an email, guesses at freight and duty, and picks one. Nothing of that decision is written down in a form anyone can check.

why it stays slow

Suppliers answer phones, not APIs. Price is never price — it is MOQ, price breaks, incoterms, freight, duty and carrying cost. And "we can do 3 weeks" is a claim, not a fact.

the cost

Days of buyer time per shortage, and a line-stop risk that is decided by whoever picked up the phone first.

a shortage, as the ERP sees it
part 6204-2RS bearing
on hand 180 pcs
take rate 40 / day
cover 4.5 days
open PO slipped +18 d
stops BOM line ASM-118

Every number above is derivable. The next twelve days of phone calls are not.

"The data to spot the shortage is already in the ERP. What is missing is everything after: who can actually supply it, at what real landed cost, and by when."

Do not linger. The judges know procurement is painful — you are here for the how.

The solution

The decision is code. So an engineer can make it.

Devin is the procurement engineer. CALL-E is one of its tools. The deliverable is a pull request a buyer merges — case, claims, transcripts, cost model, executable policy checks.

1 · autonomous, not assisted

A reorder-point scan or one button opens the case and programmatically starts a Devin session. No human writes the prompt.

2 · it checks its own work

Two pytest suites per case — the compliance policy and the landed-cost model. Both green before the PR opens.

3 · the artifact is the datastore

cases/<id>/ in Git: case.yaml, claims/*.json, transcripts, cost_model.py, decision.md. Approve by merge.

everyone else demos "an agent that calls a supplier" we demo the layer above it

"The differentiator: the output of our agent isn't a transcript or a summary. It's a diff. Reviewable, testable, revertible."

How it works

Five stages, one case file.

01

Detect

Bin, take rate, the BOM line that stops, the slipped PO. The shortage is derived, never typed.

02

Screen

Approved suppliers checked against the company's own compliance rules — as executable policy, not prose.

03

Call

CALL-E phones the ones that pass with a fixed answer sheet. Unknown is a first-class answer.

04

Cost

Price breaks, MOQ, freight, duty, carrying cost — every single-source and split plan.

05

Publish

Plans ranked on-time first, then cheapest. Opens a PR. Orders nothing.

$ POST /flow/run case=CASE-001 hold_for=SUP-KBY
screened 6 · called 4 · priced 11 plans 0.4s
$ POST /flow/call supplier=SUP-KBY live=true

The deterministic backend runs a whole case in under a second, so the pitch never waits on a spinner — and one supplier is deliberately left uncalled for the live moment.

"Detect, screen, call, cost, publish. The agent ranks and stops — a buyer picks. Nothing is ordered by the machine."

The proof

Why you should believe the demo.

116 tests, plus a UI typecheck, in one command. None of them touch the network.
2 → 1 ERP adapters — YAML mock and SQLite — behind one interface and the same test suite. "Swap in a real ERPNext" is one class.
< 1s to screen suppliers, cost every single-source and split plan, and write the review package.
0 orders placed by the agent, and zero raw phone numbers in any API response — there is a test that scans every endpoint.
money is Decimal, serialized as string — never a float "unknown" is a first-class answer, a garbled call is a confidence-0 claim live calling needs LIVE_CALLS=yes-place-real-calls, never a fallback

"The safety rails are not slideware — they are tests. A plan that misses the line stop is shown as too late however cheap it is."

Our vision

Every operational decision, reviewable as a diff.

now

Sourcing a shortage. One case, one PR, one merge.

next

The whole buyer's desk: expediting, dual-sourcing, price-break renegotiation, supplier qualification — same case file, same review gate.

the thesis

Operations runs on decisions nobody can audit. Put them in Git and an agent can be held to the same standard as an engineer: tests, review, revert.

Procurement is the wedge because the artifact is small, the money is obvious, and the incumbent workflow is a phone call.

"Procurement is where we start, not where this ends. Anything an ops team decides in a meeting can be a pull request instead."

Who buys this

Mid-market discrete manufacturers, direct materials.

the buyer

Head of Supply Chain at a €50m–€500m manufacturer. Has an ERP, has 3–15 buyers, has no data-science team, and feels every line stop personally.

why they say yes

The pain is a line stop, and the value is per resolved shortage — so the pilot is one case, not a transformation programme. It reads the ERP they already have.

wedge → land → expand

Shortage firefighting (urgent, unowned) → routine RFQ cycles → supplier base management.

pricing hypothesis

Per resolved case, plus a seat for the buyer who reviews. Priced against buyer hours saved and expedite freight avoided — both of which the case file already measures.
